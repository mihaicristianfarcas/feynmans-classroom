# Adaptive & Mastery Learning Platforms: Incumbents, Evidence and Market Size (checked 26 October 2026)

All prices were read directly from the vendor's own page on **26 October 2026** unless a different date is given. Where a vendor's page is internally inconsistent or a figure is a vendor claim rather than an independent measurement, that is stated in the line itself.

## Q1. Which platforms are the real incumbents, what do they actually sell, how does each adapt, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway

Every serious incumbent in this space sells **its own curriculum wrapped in its own sequencing engine** — none is a tool that sits beside a book the learner already owns. The only adaptive product genuinely built on top of someone else's textbook is Knewton Alta, and it is sold to higher-education course adoptions, not to readers. Consumer prices cluster between roughly $4/month (Khanmigo) and $70/month (Synthesis family tier), with the dense middle at $20–$50/month; institutional prices cluster at $8–$23 per student per year (Mathspace) up to $97 per student (the 2013 RAND cost figure for Carnegie Learning's algebra curricula).

### Cited Findings

**ALEKS (McGraw Hill) — own curriculum, higher-ed and consumer**
- ALEKS describes itself as "an artificially intelligent learning and assessment system that has been used by over 50 million students for Math, Chemistry, Statistics and Accounting", with 26 years of accumulated interaction data — vendor claim, cumulative, not current enrolment — [ALEKS About](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks)
- Its adaptivity rests on Knowledge Space Theory: it places a student "within a space of trillions of feasible knowledge states" and offers only topics the student is judged ready to learn; progress is displayed as a filling "pie" — [ALEKS About](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks)
- Vendor claims the model is right about readiness "at least 90% of the time" and that in many courses the success rate is "greater than 95%" — self-reported from its own interaction logs, no independent check — [ALEKS About](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks)
- ALEKS uses periodic "Knowledge Checks" to confirm retention, which the vendor frames as retrieval practice — [ALEKS About](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks)
- Consumer pricing, checked 26 Oct 2026: $19.95 for 1 month, $49.95 for 3 months, $99.95 for 6 months, $179.95 for 12 months — [McGraw Hill ALEKS subscription page](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/program/microsites/aleks-consumer/choose-subscription.html)
- Family (multi-student) 12-month pricing: 2 students $305.90, 3 students $431.88, 4 students $539.84 — [McGraw Hill ALEKS subscription page](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/program/microsites/aleks-consumer/choose-subscription.html)
- Consumer access is **one course at a time**; the subscription unlocks the course library rather than a single title — [McGraw Hill ALEKS subscription page](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/program/microsites/aleks-consumer/choose-subscription.html)
- Buyer: parent/homework-help for consumer; the institutional business is course placement and courseware in higher ed, sold through McGraw Hill reps — [McGraw Hill Higher Ed ALEKS](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/aleks.html)

**Khan Academy + Khanmigo — free curriculum, paid AI layer**
- The split is explicit in Khanmigo's own FAQ: "The Khan Academy content library and learning platform will always remain 100% free", while Khanmigo itself requires "a monthly or annual payment" — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/)
- Khanmigo pricing, checked 26 Oct 2026: learners and families **$4/month or $44/year**; teachers **free**; districts must "request pricing" (quote-based); sales tax excluded — [Khanmigo pricing](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing)
- One parent subscription can enable Khanmigo for **up to 10 children** under 18; the subscriber must be 18+ with a **US billing address** — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/)
- Teachers cannot grant Khanmigo to students; classroom access is only "through school or district implementations" — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/)
- Khanmigo is positioned as a tutor that "doesn't just give answers" but "guides learners to find the answer themselves", layered on Khan Academy's content library across maths, humanities, coding and social studies — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/)
- The underlying Khan Academy platform is mastery-based practice over Khan's own exercise set; the AI is an additional conversational layer rather than the sequencing engine — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/)
- Buyer: districts for classroom deployment; parents for the $4/month consumer tier; teachers as free users (a distribution channel, not a revenue line) — [Khanmigo pricing](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing)

**Brilliant — own curriculum, interactive, consumer-only**
- Brilliant sells a library of "40+ courses" of interactive, puzzle-style maths, science and CS lessons; there is no textbook, no set text, and no user-supplied content — [Brilliant Premium](https://brilliant.org/premium/)
- Pricing could not be read in USD: the page geolocated the fetch and served Romanian leu — RON 75/month for an individual, RON 100/month for a family plan with 6 seats, with an annual option advertised as roughly 29% cheaper — [Brilliant Premium](https://brilliant.org/premium/) (localised; **not** a US price)
- Buyer: motivated adult and ambitious-teen self-learners paying out of pocket; no institutional or district motion was visible on the pages checked — [Brilliant Premium](https://brilliant.org/premium/)

**Art of Problem Solving — Alcumus (free, adaptive) + AoPS Online (paid courses)**
- Alcumus is a free adaptive practice system that selects problems from AoPS's own problem bank based on the student's demonstrated weaknesses — [Alcumus](https://artofproblemsolving.com/alcumus)
- AoPS Online sells live/structured courses; the Prealgebra 1 course page carries a schema.org offer price of **$465 USD** — [AoPS Prealgebra 1](https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/course/prealgebra1)
- AoPS Online is WASC-accredited and its courses are NCAA-approved, and the catalogue runs from roughly grade 5 through grade 12 maths plus contest prep, computer science and physics — [AoPS Online](https://artofproblemsolving.com/school)

**IXL — own skill tree, K-12 all subjects, parent + district**
- IXL markets itself on breadth (K-12 maths, English, science, social studies) with a per-skill mastery model rather than a course model — [IXL](https://www.ixl.com/)
- Consumer price advertised on the site, checked 26 Oct 2026: family membership "as low as $9.95/month", with monthly and annual options and per-subject or combo tiers — [IXL](https://www.ixl.com/)

**DreamBox Learning — own adaptive K-8 curriculum, now inside Discovery Education**

- Family pricing, checked 26 Oct 2026: $19.95 for 1 month, $99.95 for 6 months, $149.95 for 12 months, $325 "lifetime" — [DreamBox family pricing](https://www.dreambox.com/family/pricing)
- Individual (single-learner) pricing on the same page: $12.95 for 1 month, $59.95 for 6 months, $99.95 for 12 months, $250 lifetime; the "lifetime" tier is described as a 10-year non-renewing term rather than a true perpetual licence — [DreamBox family pricing](https://www.dreambox.com/family/pricing)
- Vendor claim: DreamBox is "the only dual-discipline program rated STRONG by Evidence for ESSA" — company claim about a third-party rating, not itself a study — [DreamBox family pricing](https://www.dreambox.com/family/pricing)
- Buyer: elementary parents for home use; districts for core/supplemental elementary maths — [DreamBox family pricing](https://www.dreambox.com/family/pricing)

**Carnegie Learning MATHia — own curriculum, institutional only**
- MATHia is a 1:1 adaptive maths coach for middle and high school that sequences problems against Carnegie Learning's own textbook/curriculum, with teacher dashboards — [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)
- **No public price exists**; MATHia is sold by quote through district procurement — [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)
- Vendor claim: a "gold standard" RCT funded by the US Department of Education, run by RAND, covering 18,000+ students in 147 schools across 7 states, with "nearly doubled" growth in year two; the page also cites a 2021 EMERALDS study — [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)
- Buyer: district maths directors; adoption is usually bundled with Carnegie textbooks and professional development — [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)

**Synthesis — own curriculum, narrow age band, consumer-only**

- A second variant appears on the same page — Family "up to 7" at $29/month or $119/year — and a 20% "holiday sale" is applied on top; there is no single "the price" to quote — [Synthesis pricing](https://www.synthesis.com/pricing)
- Scale claim conflicts within the company's own copy: 35,000 families in one place, 25,000 in another — [Synthesis pricing](https://www.synthesis.com/pricing)
- Buyer: homeschooling and "gifted supplement" parents; no district motion visible — [Synthesis pricing](https://www.synthesis.com/pricing)

**Math Academy — the closest analogue to a mastery product, priced per student**
- Math Academy sells a fully sequenced mastery curriculum with a knowledge graph and scheduled review, at **$49/month or $500/year per student**, with a 30-day guarantee — [Math Academy](https://mathacademy.com/)
- Buyer: parents of accelerated students; the pitch is explicit acceleration through a fixed sequence, not support beside arbitrary material — [Math Academy](https://mathacademy.com/)

**Mathspace — own curriculum, explicitly cheap institutional pricing**
- Mathspace publishes per-student annual pricing: **Classes $8, Schools $15, Schools Plus $23 per student per year (USD)**, with free personal accounts — [Mathspace premium](https://mathspace.co/us/about-premium)
- Buyer: schools and districts, at price points that undercut every consumer product by an order of magnitude — [Mathspace premium](https://mathspace.co/us/about-premium)
- Note: a Mathspace blog post seen earlier described school-wide pricing at **$5 per student per year**, which would contradict the $8/$15/$23 tiers above; the URL was not retained, so that lower figure is recorded as unverified (see Gaps) — [Mathspace premium](https://mathspace.co/us/about-premium)

**Knewton Alta (Wiley) — the only adaptive layer built on a publisher's textbook**
- Knewton Alta is adaptive courseware for higher-ed STEM, built course-by-course on Wiley textbooks; there are 20+ maths/statistics titles plus chemistry, biology, physics, psychology and economics — [Knewton Alta courses](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/learning/teaching-resources/courseware/knewton-alta/courses)
- Pricing, checked 26 Oct 2026: **$49.95** single term single-course access, **$99.95** AltaPass (all courses in one discipline for 2 years), **$14.95/month** subscription — [Knewton Alta affordability](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/learning/teaching-resources/courseware/knewton-alta/affordability)
- Buyer: the **instructor or department** who adopts the title; the student pays but does not choose — [Knewton Alta affordability](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/learning/teaching-resources/courseware/knewton-alta/affordability)

### Inferences

- The market is organised around **content ownership, not around adaptation**. ALEKS, IXL, DreamBox, MATHia, Math Academy, AoPS and Brilliant all monetise a curriculum they wrote; adaptivity is the retention feature, not the product. A product that adapts _someone else's_ book has no direct incumbent at consumer price points.
- Knewton Alta proves the "adaptive layer on a textbook" model is commercially viable, but only where the publisher owns the textbook and the instructor makes the purchase. That is a structural reason a book-centred consumer product is a genuinely different slot, not a crowded one.
- Khan Academy sets the consumer price anchor for _content_ at zero and for _AI help_ at $4/month. Any paid consumer product in this category is therefore selling something Khan does not give away — books, structure, or offline ownership — rather than "maths practice".
- The institutional price band ($8–$23 per student per year at Mathspace) sits roughly one to two orders of magnitude below the consumer band ($240–$540 per year at ALEKS, DreamBox, AoPS, Math Academy). These are different businesses with different buyers, and a consumer-priced product cannot win a district tender on price.
- Synthesis's contradictory price list and contradictory family counts on a single page suggest its public numbers are marketing artefacts maintained by different teams rather than audited figures. Any market sizing that leans on vendor-published user counts in this category should be treated as directional.

**Where each incumbent leaves room for a book-centred product** (all inference, from the cited findings above):
- **ALEKS** — sells its own fixed course, one at a time; a reader cannot bring a book, and progress is an ALEKS pie, not a record attached to the material being read. The $99.95–$179.95 annual price is the ceiling a book-centred product can price against.
- **Khan Academy / Khanmigo** — the strongest free substitute for _practice_, and the hardest to charge against; but it has no concept of the user's own book, and its AI is a chat pane, not a margin note bound to a page. The NBER result says its AI adds little over its own non-AI practice, which weakens the "AI tutor" as a paid differentiator.
- **Brilliant** — interactive puzzles with no long-form reading at all; it competes for attention, not for the reading habit.
- **AoPS / Alcumus** — deep but narrow (contest-bound, grade 5-12, $465 a course); Alcumus is free practice over AoPS's own problem bank and cannot be pointed at another text.
- **IXL** — a per-skill drill tree across all subjects; the thinnest evidence in the set (+0.08, one 545-student study), and nothing that supports sustained reading.
- **DreamBox** — elementary only, gamified, and its evidence is a single 2013 K-1 study; it cannot serve an older reader.
- **MATHia** — the best-evidenced product, but institutional-only, quote-priced, and locked to Carnegie's textbooks; no consumer can buy it.
- **Synthesis** — ages 5-11 only, with unstable pricing; it is a supplement for a narrow band, not a system of record for a learner.
- **Math Academy** — the closest competitor on substance (knowledge graph plus scheduled review, $49/month), but it insists on its own sequence and offers no place for a book the reader already owns.
- **Mathspace** — $8–$23 per student per year makes it a commodity purchase for schools; it sets no consumer expectation.
- **Knewton Alta** — proves the textbook-plus-adaptive-layer model, but only for Wiley's own titles, sold through instructors. It is the clearest evidence that the "book plus adaptive margin" shape works commercially, and the clearest evidence that nobody has built it for books the reader already owns.

### Gaps

- **Khan Academy's current learner scale could not be verified.** `khanacademy.org/about`, `khanacademy.org/news`, `annualreport.khanacademy.org`, `khanacademy.org/schools/pricing` and `districts.khanacademy.org/khanmigo` all failed to render ("A required part of this site couldn't load") on 26 Oct 2026, and search engines were unusable. The widely repeated "150 million learners" figure is therefore **not cited here**.
- **No verified USD list price for Brilliant.** The page served Romanian leu because of the fetching location. Secondary sources quote $24.99/month and about $13.49/month billed annually ($161.88/year), but these could not be confirmed against the vendor page.
- **No public price for MATHia**, and no public per-student price for ALEKS institutional or Khanmigo districts — all three are quote-only.
- **AoPS scale is unknown**: no enrolment, revenue or Alcumus-problem-count figure is published on the pages checked.
- IXL's advertised "as low as $9.95/month" is a marketing floor; the page did not expose the full tier structure in a form that could be captured, so the ceiling of the family plan is unverified.
- **IXL's user and district counts are unverifiable as dated facts.** Figures of 15 million students / 95 of the top 100 districts (February 2024), 17 million / 96 districts, and 18 million students all circulate, but each is attributed to a different IXL page and the specific URLs were not retained, so no IXL scale number is cited in this document.
- **Mathspace's $5 per student per year figure** appeared in a Mathspace blog post that was not re-fetched, and it conflicts with the $8/$15/$23 tiers on the vendor's own premium page. The premium-page tiers are the cited price; the $5 figure should be treated as unresolved.

## Q2. What does independent evidence actually show, and which claims are vendor-authored?

### Takeaway

The independent evidence base is **thin, old, and modest in size**. Across four well-known products the credible effects run from roughly +0.03 to +0.20 standard deviations, with several null results; only one study (RAND's 2013 algebra trial) is large and randomised, and it is about a _predecessor_ of today's MATHia, not MATHia. Every "gold standard" or "proven" claim on a vendor page traces back to one of these few studies, or to nothing.

### Cited Findings

**Carnegie Learning / MATHia — the strongest study, and a caution about what it measured**
- The RAND research brief (18 Nov 2013) reports a randomised trial of Carnegie Learning's algebra curricula in **147 schools, 51 districts, 7 states**, with about **18,700 high-school** and **6,800 middle-school** students — [RAND RB9746](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9746.html)
- Year one produced **no significant difference** in algebra achievement; year two produced an effect of about **0.20 standard deviations** in high school, roughly moving the median student from the 50th to the 58th percentile — [RAND RB9746](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9746.html)
- The middle-school effect was similar in direction but **not statistically significant** — [RAND RB9746](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9746.html)
- The intervention cost about **$97 per student**, against about **$28 per student** for the curricula it replaced — [RAND RB9746](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9746.html)
- Carnegie Learning's current page describes this as a "gold standard" DOE-funded RAND RCT and claims "nearly doubled growth" in year two — a vendor gloss on a 0.20 SD year-two effect — [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)
- **Important distinction:** the RAND trial evaluated "Cognitive Tutor Algebra I" (CTAI), Carnegie Learning's 2013-era product, not the current MATHia. The vendor presents the result as if it were about the product on sale in 2026 — [RAND RB9746](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9746.html); [Carnegie Learning MATHia](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/mathia)

**IXL — a "strong" rating resting on one small study**
- Evidence for ESSA rates IXL Math **"strong"**, based on **one study** with **545 students**, reporting an average effect of about **+0.08** — [Evidence for ESSA: IXL Math](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/ixl-math/)
- The underlying study is a cluster randomised trial in Holland, Michigan, in spring 2023: Star Math showed **+0.13** (significant) and the state M-STEP test showed **+0.03** (not significant) — [Evidence for ESSA: IXL Math](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/ixl-math/)
- Buyer-relevant reading: a +0.08 average effect on one 545-student study is below the threshold most districts would call transformative, yet it is the best-rated evidence any consumer maths product in this set can show — [Evidence for ESSA: IXL Math](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/ixl-math/)

**DreamBox — one 2013 WWC-reviewed study, narrow population**
- The What Works Clearinghouse intervention report (December 2013) found **"potentially positive effects"** for DreamBox Learning, based on **one study** — [WWC evidence snapshot](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/EvidenceSnapshot/627)
- That study covered **557 K-1 students** in a charter school in San Jose, California, with **88%** eligible for free/reduced-price lunch and **81%** English learners — a very specific population, and a very early grade band — [WWC evidence snapshot](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/EvidenceSnapshot/627)
- DreamBox's "only dual-discipline program rated STRONG by Evidence for ESSA" claim is a company statement about a third-party rating and is not accompanied by the effect size on the pricing page — [DreamBox family pricing](https://www.dreambox.com/family/pricing)

**Khanmigo — a large, recent, independent RCT with a small effect and an engagement story**
- NBER Working Paper 35620 (August 2026) reports a **two-year cluster randomised trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools** — [Stanford SCALE summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/one-click-away-ai-tutoring-khanmigo-two-year-school-experiment)
- Estimated effect: about **+1.3 national percentile ranks per term**, or roughly **0.06–0.08 SD per year**, rising to about **0.14 SD** for students who participated fully — [Stanford SCALE summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/one-click-away-ai-tutoring-khanmigo-two-year-school-experiment)
- The gains "resemble" those from Khan Academy practice **without** the AI, i.e. the AI layer did not clearly beat the free product it sits on — [Stanford SCALE summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/one-click-away-ai-tutoring-khanmigo-two-year-school-experiment)
- **96%** of students tried Khanmigo, but the median student messaged it on only **a third of practice days** and in **17%** of sessions where they got an error — engagement, not model quality, is the binding constraint — [Stanford SCALE summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/one-click-away-ai-tutoring-khanmigo-two-year-school-experiment)

**ALEKS, Brilliant, Synthesis, AoPS, Mathspace, Math Academy — no independent efficacy evidence located**
- No RCT, WWC review, Evidence for ESSA entry or peer-reviewed evaluation was found for Brilliant, Synthesis, AoPS/Alcumus, Mathspace or Math Academy on the sources checked; their efficacy claims are self-reported — [Brilliant Premium](https://brilliant.org/premium/); [Synthesis pricing](https://www.synthesis.com/pricing); [AoPS](https://artofproblemsolving.com/); [Mathspace](https://mathspace.co/us/about-premium); [Math Academy](https://mathacademy.com/)
- ALEKS publishes no independent efficacy study on its own site; its evidence page relies on the learning-rate statistic above, derived from its own logs — [ALEKS About](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks)

### Inferences

- The honest summary of this category's evidence is: **effects exist but are small (≈0.03–0.20 SD) and fragile**, and the largest, cleanest trial in the set (RAND) is now 13 years old and tested a product that is no longer sold under that name.
- The pattern in the Khanmigo RCT — a real but small effect that does not clearly exceed the non-AI product, with usage as the limiting factor — is the most decision-relevant finding for any product in this space, because it says the marginal value is in **getting the learner to actually work**, not in the sophistication of the sequencing.
- Where a vendor's own materials conflict about its evidence tier (IXL's ESSA page reportedly claiming Tier 1 while its ESSA study PDF says Tier 2), the marketing claim should be treated as unverified even when the underlying study is real — though this specific contradiction is itself uncited here, so verify before printing it.
- The absence of evidence for Brilliant, Synthesis and AoPS should not be read as evidence of absence of effect; these are consumer products whose efficacy has simply never been independently tested in public.

### Gaps

- **ALEKS effect sizes could not be retrieved.** A meta-analysis of ALEKS (reported as 15 studies, 24 effect sizes, 2005–2015, with larger effects for shorter use) exists behind a Taylor & Francis paywall that returned HTTP 403; no effect size, study list or URL is verified here, so do not cite a number for ALEKS from it.
- **No independent evaluation of ALEKS in K-12** was located, despite ALEKS being one of the two oldest adaptive systems in the market.
- **No independent evaluation of Math Academy, Mathspace, Brilliant, Synthesis or AoPS** was located. Whether such studies exist behind paywalls or in district reports is unknown.
- **MATHia's own (post-2013) evidence base is unverified.** The vendor cites a 2021 EMERALDS study but the citation was not followed to a primary source, so its design and size are unknown.
- Evidence for ESSA's DreamBox entry was not read directly; only DreamBox's marketing paraphrase of it is captured here.
- **The IXL Tier 1 vs Tier 2 contradiction is unverified.** IXL's ESSA page appeared to claim Tier 1 while IXL's own ESSA study PDF described Tier 2, but the exact URLs were not retained in this session. Don't print this as fact without re-checking ixl.com's ESSA pages.

## Q3. How big is this market, and what does the size evidence leave open for a book-centred product?

### Takeaway

Syndicated estimates of the "adaptive learning market" cluster around **$5–5.8B for 2025** but diverge by about **2.3× on the forecast** and disagree on basic structure (cloud vs on-premises, K-12 share). The defensible statement is a **single-digit-billion-dollar global market in 2025 growing at roughly 19–21% a year**, not a specific number — and note that these totals include corporate training, so the K-12 maths slice is materially smaller than any headline figure.

### Cited Findings

- Grand View Research: adaptive learning was **$3.46 billion in 2022**, growing at a **21.4% CAGR from 2023 to 2030**; last updated December 2023, so this is the oldest of the three and its 2025 value is an extrapolation, not a measurement — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/adaptive-learning-market-report)
- Mordor Intelligence: **$5.13 billion in 2025**, forecast **$12.66 billion by 2030**, **19.77% CAGR**; the page states its figures come from a "proprietary estimation framework" updated January 2026 and was last updated 11 September 2026 — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market)
- IMARC: **$5.8 billion in 2025**, forecast **$29.7 billion by 2034**, **19.30% CAGR** for 2026–2034; last updated 7 July 2026 — [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/adaptive-learning-market)
- The two 2025 estimates differ by about **13%** ($5.13B vs $5.8B), but their forecasts diverge much more: Mordor reaches $12.66B by 2030 while IMARC reaches $29.7B by 2034 — a roughly **2.3×** gap at the endpoint even after allowing for the four extra years — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market); [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/adaptive-learning-market)
- The same two reports contradict each other on structure: Mordor puts **cloud at 71.3%** of 2024 revenue and **K-12 at 42.5%**, while IMARC says **on-premises leads** and that **academic is 78.5%** of 2025 revenue — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market); [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/adaptive-learning-market)
- Both agree North America is the largest region but disagree on its size: Mordor **44.6%** of 2024 revenue, IMARC **over 37.5%** in 2025 — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market); [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/adaptive-learning-market)
- Both list substantially the same vendors as the market leaders: DreamBox, McGraw Hill/ALEKS, Knewton/Wiley, Carnegie Learning, Mathspace, Pearson, Curriculum Associates — i.e. the platform list in Q1 _is_ the market these reports are sizing — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market); [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/adaptive-learning-market)
- Mordor records McGraw Hill's **$537 million IPO** (July 2025) as evidence of incumbent appetite for AI-platform expansion — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/adaptive-learning-market)

### Inferences

- Reporting a single market size for this category would be false precision. The defensible framing for a market document is: **~$3.5B (2022, Grand View) rising to ~$5–6B (2025, Mordor/IMARC), growing ~19–21% a year**, with the caveat that the 2.3× forecast divergence reflects genuinely different assumptions rather than rounding.
- Because Mordor puts K-12 at 42.5% of a $5.13B 2025 market and IMARC puts "academic" (K-12 plus higher ed) at 78.5% of $5.8B, the K-12 slice is somewhere between roughly **$2.2B and $4.5B** on the vendors' own numbers — before narrowing to maths only, and before excluding corporate training, which both reports include.
- The structural disagreement (cloud vs on-premises leading) is a useful tell: these reports are built from vendor lists and top-down assumptions, not from audited revenue, so none of them should be used to size a niche.
- The absence of any local-first, files-based, book-centred entrant from either report's vendor list suggests the category is either too small to be tracked or not yet recognised as a category — both of which are consistent with a genuinely open slot rather than a crowded one.
- The consumer price data in Q1 gives a more reliable sizing input than the syndicated totals: the addressable consumer spends **$4–$70/month**, and the modal successful product (ALEKS, DreamBox, Math Academy) sits at **$20–$50/month**.

### Gaps

- **No credible, methodologically transparent estimate of the K-12 _maths software_ segment specifically** was obtained. The figures seen in search results ranged from about $2.4B to $18.4B for overlapping definitions, but the landing pages either blocked fetching (HTTP 403 from Fortune Business Insights and MarketsandMarkets) or were visible only as snippets, so no number for that segment is cited here.
- **No revenue, subscriber or enrolment figures** were found for any of the consumer businesses (ALEKS, DreamBox, IXL, Brilliant, Synthesis, AoPS, Math Academy), so the market cannot be sized bottom-up from vendor disclosures.
- **No data on willingness to pay for a book-centred product**, and no evidence on whether buyers of ALEKS/DreamBox would substitute a book-plus-adaptive-layer product. This is the central unknown for the product thesis and was outside what public sources could answer.
- Khan Academy's scale — the single most important number for calibrating how much of this market is served for free — remains unverified (see Q1 gaps).
