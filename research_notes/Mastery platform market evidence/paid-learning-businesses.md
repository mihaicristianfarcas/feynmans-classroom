# Businesses that get paid for structured learning and retention (outside the US university lecture hall)

Research notes, checked 20–26 October 2026. Scope: businesses whose product is essentially **prepared material + a habit**, plus the household-spend anchors needed to judge willingness to pay. All prices are as displayed on the date checked; currency is local unless converted, and every conversion is flagged as approximate. **[Company claim]** marks a figure the business publishes about itself; **[independent]** marks third-party, regulatory or academic data; **[secondary]** marks an aggregator or review site that is useful but not primary. Derived figures show the arithmetic and are labelled as inference, not fact.

---

## 1. Kumon and Mathnasium: scale, price, retention, and what parents actually buy

### Takeaway

These are the clearest existing proof that families pay **monthly, for years, for routine and prepared worksheets** — not for explanations. Price is $130–220 per subject per month in the US, £70 in the UK and €111 in Germany at Kumon, and roughly $350–580 per month at Mathnasium; that is **$1,500–6,000 per child per year**, roughly **20–85× a €60/year app**. Both are growing at the centre level. Neither discloses student retention, and the only hard churn number in the public record is franchisee turnover, not family churn.

### Cited Findings

**Kumon scale**
- Kumon operates **23,700 learning centres, 3.6 million students, 21,800 instructors in 62 countries** — [Kumon franchising site, German edition](https://www.kumonfranchising.com/de-DE) [company claim, accessed 26 Oct 2026]. A secondary aggregator gives 23,400 centres / 3.58M students as of September 2025 — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Kumon) [secondary, AI-generated, treat as corroboration only].
- Kumon has **1,689 US units** (1,671 franchised, 18 company-owned) — [freefdds Kumon vs Mathnasium, 2026 FDD data](https://freefdds.com/compare/kumon-vs-mathnasium) [secondary aggregation of the 2026 FDD].
- Kumon **does not publish an Item 19 Financial Performance Representation**, so there is no disclosed average centre revenue — same source.

**Kumon price**
- US: **$130–220 per subject per month**, one-off registration $50–80, materials $10–30; seven centres checked 3–5 Oct 2026 — [Tutero](https://www.tutero.com/us/blog/kumon-cost) and [Superprof](https://www.superprof.com/blog/kumon-cost) [secondary, but both list named centres and dates]. Superprof’s named examples: Long Island City NY $140, Leominster MA $145, Piscataway NJ $150, Wylie TX $150, Santa Ana East CA $170, Houston Garden Oaks $190, Prospect Heights Brooklyn $210 — same source.
- UK: **“a fixed monthly fee of £70 per subject and £35 registration fee”** — [Kumon UK official Facebook video](https://www.facebook.com/Kumon.UK/videos/book-a-free-assessment/425608540628628/) [company claim]; a Kumon Loughborough centre post repeats “starting at just £70 per subject per month” — [centre Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/kumonloughborough/posts/starting-at-just-70-per-subject-per-month-kumon-is-one-of-the-most-competitive-t/1281057260189175/) [company claim]. Third-party review puts UK at **£70–85/month per subject, ~£40 registration** — [Maths Insider](https://www.mathsinsider.com/8-things-to-hate-about-kumon-a-review/) [secondary].
- Germany: **€111 per month per subject** plus a one-off registration fee, at Kumon Neuss-Furth — [Kumon Neuss-Furth centre page](https://www.kumon.de/neuss-furth) [company claim, checked 26 Oct 2026].
- Australia: **A$160 per month per subject**, diagnostic test free — [Kumon Australia monthly fee page](https://au.kumonglobal.com/monthly-fee) [company claim].

**Kumon economics and rules**
- Royalty is **$36 per full-paying student per month**, rising to **$38 on 1 January 2026** ($19 for partially exempt/prorated students) — [1851 Franchise summary of the Kumon FDD](https://1851franchise.com/kumon-franchise-costs-fees-profit-and-data-for-2025-2729462) [secondary, quoting the FDD].
- Kumon has a **20% annual franchisee turnover/closure rate** — [freefdds](https://freefdds.com/compare/kumon-vs-mathnasium) [secondary]. This is **franchisee churn, not student churn**.
- Cancellation requires **30 days’ notice** in the US — [Ruvimo](https://www.ruvimo.com/blog/kumon-cost-price-guide) [secondary]. In Australia, notice must reach the centre by the **15th of the preceding month**, or the next month is payable — [Kumon Australia](https://au.kumonglobal.com/monthly-fee) [company claim].
- The method is **daily 30-minute worksheets, two centre visits per week, self-paced progression**, explicitly positioned against “tuition or classroom learning” — [Kumon UK helpdesk](https://www.kumon.co.uk/helpdesk) and [Kumon US](https://www.kumon.com) [company claim].

**Mathnasium scale**
- **1,256 centres worldwide at end-2025**, up from 1,189 at the start of 2025; **1,000+ in the US, 100+ in Canada**, remainder across 10 other international markets; **88 new locations opened and 121 new franchise agreements in 2025**; systemwide growth **6% year on year** — [1851 Franchise, Jan 2026](https://1851franchise.com/mathnasium/mathnasium-2025-record-growth-romania-2026-expansion-2731372) [secondary, based on company release].
- Company release: **“over 1,200 locations across 12 countries”**, No. 83 on Entrepreneur Franchise 500 — [PR Newswire, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mathnasium-kicks-off-2026-with-strong-expansion-momentum-top-franchise-rankings-and-a-sharpened-digital-experience-302670920.html) [company claim].
- 2026 FDD discloses **average centre revenue $293,590**, payback 3.8–5.8 years; royalty **10% of revenue + 2% ad fund**; franchise fee $49,000 — [freefdds](https://freefdds.com/compare/kumon-vs-mathnasium) [secondary, quoting the FDD].

**Mathnasium price**
- Six US centres checked October 2026: **entry plans $350–499/month, unlimited up to $579/month**, enrollment fee $198–199 — [Superprof](https://www.superprof.com/blog/mathnasium-cost) [secondary, named centres].
- Wider reported range **$250–450/month**, ~$100–175 assessment — [Thinkster](https://www.hellothinkster.com/blog/mathnasium-alternative) and [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/mathnasium-cost) [secondary].
- Sessions are **60 minutes (pre-algebra and below) or 90 minutes (Algebra 1+)**, 2–3 visits per week recommended — [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/mathnasium-cost) [secondary].

### Inferences

- **Kumon is a curriculum, not tutoring.** The parent pays for a sequence, a daily quota and a centre habit; instruction is deliberately minimal. Mathnasium adds diagnosis and small-group teaching, which is why it costs roughly 2–3× Kumon.
- **Derived average students per Mathnasium centre:** $293,590 average revenue ÷ ($400/month × 12 = $4,800) ≈ **61 students**; at $300/month ≈ **82 students**. Both are plausible for an after-school centre and imply the unit economics depend on ~60–80 recurring families, not thousands of one-off users.
- **Derived Kumon HQ revenue per US student:** $38/month × 12 = **$456/year** in royalty alone. If the royalty is ~20–25% of tuition, implied tuition is ~$1,800–2,300/year — consistent with the observed $150–190/month price. This is a useful benchmark: the franchisor’s cut alone is **~6.5–7× a €60/year app** (at an assumed €1 ≈ $1.16).
- **Price ceiling for a parent who has already decided to pay for structure:** £840/year (Kumon UK, one subject) to ~$5,000/year (Mathnasium, one child). €60/year is not in this category at all; it is a rounding error against it.
- **What they sell that a chatbot cannot:** the timetable, the worksheet sequence, the weekly trip, the instructor’s judgement that “you are ready to move on”, and a visible progress ritual. None of these require better explanations.

### Gaps

- **No public student-level retention or average tenure for either chain.** FDDs disclose franchisee turnover (Kumon 20%/year) but not family churn; Kumon does not disclose Item 19 at all. The 20% figure must not be used as a student churn proxy.
- No reliable current Kumon UK fee schedule: Kumon UK says fees vary by centre and routes parents to a centre finder, so the £70 figure is an undated marketing claim, not a national tariff.
- No independent audit of Mathnasium’s $293,590 average (self-reported by franchisees in Item 19).

## 2. Price points that survive in each category, and the annual ceiling

### Takeaway

There are four distinct price bands, and they are separated by roughly 10× steps: **commodity study tools $36–180/year; single-subject exam prep £180–540/year; school per-seat licences £5–15/student/year; human centres $1,500–6,000/year.** €60/year sits at the **top of the commodity band**, not the exam-prep band. The reliable high-stakes payment trigger is not “explanations” but **a dated exam and a grade guarantee**.

### Cited Findings

**Commodity / retention tools**
- Quizlet Plus: **$35.99/year** ($2.99/month equivalent) or $7.99/month; Plus Unlimited **$44.99/year** — [Quizlet upgrade page](https://quizlet.com/upgrade) [company claim, checked 26 Oct 2026], corroborated by [Brighterly, March 2026](https://brighterly.com/blog/quizlet-cost) [secondary].
- AnkiHub: **Core $6/month or $55–66/year; Premium $10/month; Lifetime $450** — [AnkiHub home](https://www.ankihub.net) [company claim, checked 26 Oct 2026]. A review site records the same tiers and notes the price rose from $5 — [StudyCardsAI, Sept 2026](https://studycardsai.com/blog/ankihub-guide) [secondary].

**UK exam prep (self-serve, one subject)**
- Up Learn: **£429.99 total “until summer 2027 exams”**; monthly **£104.99/month**; a parent page still quotes “from £69.99/month” — [Up Learn pricing](https://uplearn.co.uk/pricing) and [Up Learn for parents](https://uplearn.co.uk/resource-articles/for-parents) [company claim, checked 26 Oct 2026]. A school flyer says each module “typically costs £299” direct, £125 through the college — [KEDST/Up Learn flyer, Oct 2024](https://kedst.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/All-Up-Learn-and-KEDST-flyers.pdf) [company claim].
- Tassomai: private family subscription **£8.99/month (Years 3–6) to £44.99/month (Years 9–11)**, 7-day trial, no contract; school-parent add-ons £2.99/month for extra subjects and **£3.99/month for “Mai Unlimited”** — [Iconic review](https://iconicblogs.co.uk/tassomai) [secondary] and [Tassomai school-parents page](https://www.tassomai.com/school-parents) [company claim].
- Seneca: price is shown only behind login, but a Trustpilot reviewer reports a **£179.88 annual charge on 3 October 2026** — [Trustpilot Seneca](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com) [user report, not company price]. Seneca also sells a **“Seneca Guarantee” for an extra £5.99/month** — [Seneca help centre](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3746290-how-much-is-premium) [company claim].
- Save My Exams: aggregator records **Essential $8/month and Premium $16/month**, with annual pricing “priced on request” — [Everything Xiaomi, checked 2 Oct 2026](https://everythingxiaomi.com/apps/save-my-exams/pricing) [secondary]. Save My Exams’ own join page confirms annual subscriptions auto-renew and warns that cancelling on the end date may be too late — [Save My Exams join](https://www.savemyexams.com/join) [company claim].

**School per-seat**
- Tassomai: **£15 per student per year for one cohort, falling to £5 per student if a school buys Years 7–11**; up to two core subjects included — [EdTech Impact comparison](https://edtechimpact.com/compare/century-tech-app-vs-tassomai) and [BESA LendED](https://www.lended.org.uk/product/tassomai) [secondary, quoting Tassomai’s published pricing].
- Dr Frost Maths: **£650 + VAT per school per year**, unlimited teachers and students; **free for independent learners**; 60-day trial; 25% or 50% discounts for eligible schools — [Dr Frost pricing](https://www.drfrost.org/pricing) and [getting-started PDF](https://www.drfrost.org/docs/DrFrost-GettingStartedForTeachers.pdf) [company claim, checked 26 Oct 2026]. Dr Frost Learning is a **registered charity** (no. 1194954) — same source.

**Derived annual equivalents**
- Up Learn £429.99 for access to summer 2027 exams: if that is ~24 months, **≈£215/year**; if ~18 months, **≈£287/year**. (Arithmetic, not a company figure.)
- Tassomai Y9–11 at £44.99/month: **£539.88/year** if paid for 12 months; **≈£405** for a 9-month exam-year subscription. (Arithmetic.)
- Seneca £179.88/year (user-reported).
- Quizlet $35.99/year; AnkiHub $66/year (annual plan on the Step Deck page) or $55/year (MCAT page).
- Kumon UK £70 × 12 = **£840/year per subject**; Germany €111 × 12 = **€1,332/year per subject**; US $130–220 × 12 = **$1,560–2,640/year per subject**.
- Mathnasium $350–499 × 12 = **$4,200–5,988/year** at entry level.

### Inferences

- **The ceiling is category-specific, not student-specific.** A household that pays £840/year for Kumon has demonstrated it will pay 14× €60/year for routine. A household that pays £430 for one Up Learn A-level has demonstrated it will pay ~7× €60/year for a grade guarantee. The €60 figure is only credible as a **self-serve, no-guarantee, low-stakes** price.
- **Price is anchored to the alternative, not to cost.** Up Learn explicitly anchors to “teachers paid £100+/hour for 1:1 tuition” and to the cost of a bad grade — [Up Learn pricing page](https://uplearn.co.uk/pricing) [company claim]. Kumon anchors to “one of the most competitive tuition providers in the UK” at £70 — [Kumon Loughborough centre post](https://www.facebook.com/kumonloughborough/posts/starting-at-just-70-per-subject-per-month-kumon-is-one-of-the-most-competitive-t/1281057260189175/) [company claim].
- **Annual prepay is the norm at the top of the market** (Up Learn “until exams”, Save My Exams annual auto-renew, Tassomai school year, Kumon monthly with notice). The businesses are structurally annual, which fits a Course Pack sold per course/term better than a monthly app subscription.
- **A €60/year price is roughly the cost of one hour of UK GCSE tutoring** (£35–50) and less than one month of Kumon (£70 ≈ €80). If the Course Pack is sold to a teacher or department, per-seat pricing should be compared with **Tassomai’s £5–15/student/year** and **Dr Frost’s £650/school/year**, not with a student app.

### Gaps

- Seneca’s and Save My Exams’ official current prices could not be read without an account; the £179.88 and $8/$16 figures are a user report and a secondary aggregator respectively.
- No public data on what share of Up Learn or Seneca revenue comes from schools vs individuals; this matters for whether the Course Pack should be sold to teachers or students.
- No evidence found of any self-serve exam-prep product sustaining **above ~£450 per subject per exam cycle** outside 1:1 tutoring.

## 3. Is there evidence people pay for _retention_ specifically?

### Takeaway

Yes, but almost never for “spaced repetition” as a naked feature. People pay for **a maintained deck, a daily quota, or a grade guarantee**; the memory science is the justification, not the product. The strongest direct evidence is AnkiHub/AnKing in medicine, where users pay $6–10/month for a deck that is continually corrected and updated. Quizlet proves the mass market will pay ~$36/year for flashcards, but that is a commodity price.

### Cited Findings

- **AnKing Step Deck passed 100,000 cumulative subscribers on 19 January 2025**, adding 5,819 in one update window — [AnkiHub community, Update #13](https://community.ankihub.net/t/anking-step-deck-update-13/385291) [company/community claim]. A later update reports **9,370 new subscribers** in a six-week window — [Update #17](https://community.ankihub.net/t/anking-step-deck-update-17/482024) [company/community claim].
- AnkiHub’s paid tiers are **$6/month Core, $10/month Premium (AI search/chatbot), $450 lifetime**; the Step Deck annual plan is **$66/year**, the MCAT deck **$55/year** — [AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net) [company claim].
- AnkiHub’s pitch is explicitly **maintenance, not content**: “live updates catch errata and guideline changes that a static downloaded file will not” — [StudyCardsAI](https://studycardsai.com/blog/anking-deck-guide) [secondary].
- **Up Learn sells retrieval practice as a feature inside a grade guarantee**: “seamless progress tracking and retrieval practice to keep your learning fresh”, alongside “top grades guaranteed or your money back” and a 97% A*/A claim — [Up Learn](https://uplearn.co.uk) [company claim].
- **Tassomai sells the habit, not the algorithm**: a “Daily Goal”, a knowledge “Tree”, and a weekly parent email/dashboard; private subscriptions span £8.99–44.99/month — [Tassomai school parents](https://www.tassomai.com/school-parents) and [Iconic](https://iconicblogs.co.uk/tassomai) [company claim / secondary].
- **Quizlet’s paid conversion is driven by caps, not memory science**: the free tier caps Learn/Test rounds; Plus removes the caps — [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/quizlet-cost) [secondary]. That is a convenience purchase at $35.99/year.
- **No major consumer product was found that sells FSRS/spaced repetition alone at a premium.** Anki itself is free and open source; the paid layer is the maintained deck and sync/update service.

### Inferences

- **Willingness to pay for retention is real but bounded by the “deck” metaphor.** Users pay for a thing that is kept correct and complete (AnkiHub), or for a daily obligation (Tassomai, Kumon), or for an outcome guarantee (Up Learn). “FSRS inside” is not a price argument on its own.
- **A Course Pack is closer to AnkiHub than to Quizlet**: it is a maintained, correct, exam-aligned artifact. That supports a **$50–100/year per course** price, not $36, if the maintenance is visible (errata, syllabus changes, new figures).
- **The strongest retention pitch is negative**: “your deck will be wrong by next year unless someone maintains it.” AnkiHub’s entire paid rationale is that a static file rots. The same argument applies to a book turned into figures/quizzes: the book stays, the scaffolding needs updating.
- **Retention alone does not create the payment trigger; the exam date does.** Up Learn’s guarantee, Tassomai’s exam-year licence and Kumon’s monthly routine all attach to an external deadline. A retention product without a deadline is a $36/year commodity.

### Gaps

- AnkiHub does not publish revenue, paid conversion, or churn. “100,000 cumulative subscribers” includes free-plan users and cannot be converted into ARR without inventing a conversion rate.
- No public data on how many AnkiHub users pay annually vs monthly, or how many lapse after Step 1.
- No independent RCT evidence was found that any _paid_ consumer product improves retention versus free Anki; the evidence base is company case studies and exam-result marketing.

## 4. Who is growing, flat or collapsing in 2025–26, and why

### Takeaway

The split is brutal and instructive. **PhysicsWallah is growing profitably at low ARPU in a high-stakes market; Unacademy has collapsed into a distressed sale; Megastudy is shrinking with the Korean school-age population; the Chinese K-12 giants were legislated out of existence.** In the UK, the self-serve exam-prep layer is healthy but small; the franchise centre layer is growing slowly and steadily. The common survival factor is **either a low price point with massive volume (PW) or a prepared, exam-specific product with a guarantee (Up Learn, Tassomai)** — not generalist “learning”.

### Cited Findings

**PhysicsWallah (India) — growing, low price, now profitable at EBITDA level**
- FY26: revenue from operations **₹3,900 crore, +35% YoY**; EBITDA **₹300 crore** (tripled); PAT **−₹24 crore**; **4.9 million paid students, +20%** — [Yahoo Finance summary of Q4 FY26 call, 27 May 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PWL.BO/earnings/PWL.BO-Q4-2026-earnings_call-567556.html) [independent reporting of company results].
- 9M FY26: revenue **₹2,980.7 crore (+31%)**, **4.37 million unique paid users** (3.60M a year earlier), adjusted EBITDA **₹647.4 crore (22% margin)**, PAT **₹45 crore** after one-off expenses — [PW Q3 FY26 press release, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.pw.live/version14/assets/pdfs/feb_5_physicswallah_press_release_q3fy26_results.pdf) [company results].
- Q1 FY27: revenue **₹1,054 crore (+24% YoY, +15% QoQ)**; pre-Ind AS EBITDA **−₹44 crore (−4%)**; PBT **−₹84 crore**; online K-12 revenue **+88%**, enrollments **+41%**; NEET exam-cycle delay shifted offline revenue — [Yahoo Finance Q1 FY27 call summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/physicswallah-ltd-nse-pwl-q1-010734755.html) and [Moneycontrol, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/earnings/physicswallah-q1-net-loss-narrows-30-yoy-to-rs-88-3-crore-revenue-up-24-14005804.html) [independent reporting].
- PW says online penetration is **~2% of India’s online education market**, with 3.4M+ daily users and 106+ minutes average engagement — [PW Q3 FY26 release](https://www.pw.live/version14/assets/pdfs/feb_5_physicswallah_press_release_q3fy26_results.pdf) [company claim].

**Unacademy (India) — collapsing, sold for parts**
- FY25 total revenue **₹826.3 crore, down 16%**; net loss **₹436 crore** (narrowed 31%); EBITDA loss **₹305 crore** — [Open Magazine, citing internal documents reviewed by Entrackr](https://openthemagazine.com/business/from-344-billion-unicorn-to-200-million-unicorpse-how-real-was-unacademys-valuation) [secondary].
- Operating revenue fell from **₹907 crore in FY23 to ₹701 crore in FY25** — [Inc42 via LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/unacademy-hits-reset-again-inc42-td0rc) [secondary]. Note the discrepancy with the ₹826.3 crore “total revenue” figure: operating vs total revenue. Do not mix them.
- Unacademy was sold to **upGrad for just over $200 million in shares**, against a **$3.44 billion** peak valuation — [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/from-344-billion-unicorn-to-200-million-unicorpse-how-real-was-unacademys-valuation) [secondary]. upGrad FY26 gross revenue **₹2,000 crore**, Ind-AS EBITDA ₹123 crore, net loss ₹130 crore — same source.

**Megastudy (Korea) — shrinking with the cohort**
- Megastudy Education FY2025 revenue **KRW 884.9 billion, −6.1%** (from KRW 942.2bn); operating margin rose even as revenue fell — [Nathan R.G. analysis](https://www.nathanrg.com/insights/megastudyedu) [secondary analysis of filings].
- A different listed entity, Megastudy Co. (KOSDAQ 072870), reports **KRW 127.28 billion revenue in 2025, −5.2%**, operating profit ~$15.5M — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/kosdaq/072870) and [StocksKorea](https://stockskorea.com/stock/072870) [secondary]. **These are not the same business as Megastudy Education**; do not merge the two figures.

**China — the extreme case**
- Yuanfudao reached a **$15.5 billion valuation** after a **$2.2 billion** round in October 2020; the 2021 “double reduction” policy made its core after-school tutoring model illegal — [Quartz](https://qz.com/2029682/online-tutoring-flourished-in-anxious-china-until-beijing-stepped-in) and [CNBC, 5 Aug 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/08/05/chinas-harsh-education-crackdown-sends-parents-businesses-scrambling.html) [independent reporting]. A retrospective account puts it at 400 million+ users and 30,000+ employees before the crackdown — [Medium, Feb 2026](https://medium.com/illumination/the-15-5-billion-unicorn-that-vanished-overnight-f71a34f80a77) [low-quality secondary; use only as colour].
- China’s K-12 after-school tutoring market was **¥800 billion ($123.7bn) in 2019**, forecast to exceed ¥1 trillion by 2025 before the crackdown — [Oliver Wyman via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/08/05/chinas-harsh-education-crackdown-sends-parents-businesses-scrambling.html) [industry estimate].
- **No reliable 2025–26 financials for Yuanfudao were found.** Searches returned only pre-2021 coverage and retrospective pieces. Treat its current status as unknown rather than assuming survival or death.

**UK / US self-serve**
- Up Learn claims **685+ schools**, “1 in 3 A Level students” using it, _*97% A*/A after completing_*, and 50,000 scholarships — [Up Learn home](https://uplearn.co.uk) [company claims; the “1 in 3” figure is not independently verified and is inconsistent with 685 schools].
- StudyFetch: **6 million students in 2025**, **$11.5M Series A** led by Owl Ventures with College Board participation (June 2025), **>$10M ARR** and **7M+ students** by 2026 — [StudyFetch press release, 5 Jan 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/studyfetch-helps-millions-of-students-learn-responsibly-with-ai-in-2025-302651999.html) [company claim] and [New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-education-top-revenue) [secondary].
- StudyFetch was at **$108K MRR and 190K downloads in September 2025** — [Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/ai-study-app-hits-1b-views-over-225-accounts) [secondary]. The jump from ~$1.3M ARR to >$10M ARR within months is large; treat the later figure as a company claim until audited.
- Turbo AI (formerly TurboLearn): **5M users and eight-figure ARR in October 2025**, profitable, 20,000 new users/day — [TechCrunch, 23 Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users) [independent reporting of company claims]; **10M users by July 2026** — [Turbo AI blog](https://www.turbo.ai/blog/july-2026-announcement-10-million-users) [company claim]; **$13M lifetime revenue** — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/diana-bocco/student-founders-entrepreneurs-ai-startup-venture-capital-funding/91380311) [independent interview].

### Inferences

- **PW’s model is the price-ceiling proof for high-stakes, price-sensitive markets.** Derived ARPU: FY26 ₹3,900 crore ÷ 4.9M paid students = **₹7,959 per paid student per year** (≈$90 at an assumed ₹88/$). For 9M FY26: ₹2,980.7 crore ÷ 4.37M = **₹6,821 per paid user for nine months**. Even at that price PW earns a 22% adjusted EBITDA margin at scale — because content is reused across millions and delivery is cheap. A €60/year product in a rich country is 2–3× PW’s ARPU, not a low price.
- **Unacademy’s collapse is a warning about generalist breadth.** It sold everything to everyone (UPSC, banking, languages, upskilling), lost 16% of revenue, and exited for ~6% of its peak valuation. PW focused on exam categories and a low price, and grew.
- **Megastudy’s decline looks demographic, not competitive.** A 6% revenue decline with rising margin is what a mature test-prep incumbent looks like when the number of Korean teenagers falls. It is not a failure of the model; it is a shrinking pool.
- **China shows the political risk of being the paid default for high-stakes exam prep.** A single policy removed the entire category. A local-first, file-based product is structurally more resilient to that specific risk, but also less able to charge a national monopoly price.
- **The AI-native tools are growing on distribution, not pedagogy.** StudyFetch’s 225 TikTok/Instagram accounts and Turbo’s 20,000 users/day are acquisition machines; neither reports retention. Their ARR is real but their defensibility is not yet proven.

### Gaps

- Unacademy’s paid-user count is not disclosed, so no ARPU can be derived.
- PW’s “paid users” are **unique paid users over the period**, not average concurrent subscribers; the derived ARPU is therefore a period ARPU, not a monthly ARPU, and overstates annual ARPU if users buy short crash courses.
- Yuanfudao’s current revenue, users and ownership are unknown.
- No independent verification of Up Learn’s 685 schools or 97% claim; the “1 in 3 A-level students” claim is implausible on its face and should not be repeated as fact.

## 5. Churn, refunds and completion: the “exam burst then silence” pattern

### Takeaway

The pattern is real and the industry’s own numbers show it. The best single data point is StudyFetch celebrating that **“nearly one in six users returned for three or more study sessions”** during finals — i.e. **~83% did not**. Exam-prep businesses are built around an annual cycle, not retention: Up Learn sells access _until the exam_, Tassomai licences run _to the start of the next academic year_, and Save My Exams auto-renews annually. Vendor churn benchmarks put edtech at **3.8–7.8% monthly churn (37–62% annualised)**. There is no audited student-retention data from any of the named companies.

### Cited Findings

- **StudyFetch:** during the December 2025 finals window, **“nearly one in six users returned for three or more study sessions”**, and weekly active usage rose **more than 50%** at the start of finals — [StudyFetch press release, 5 Jan 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/studyfetch-helps-millions-of-students-learn-responsibly-with-ai-in-2025-302651999.html) [company claim, framed as a retention success].
- **EdTech churn benchmarks:** average **3.8% monthly** — [RetentionCheck, 19 May 2026](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/edtech); median **7.8% monthly** — [ChurnTools](https://churntools.com/churn-rate/edtech); both are vendor blogs, not audited data, and they conflict. Annualised: 1−(1−0.038)^12 = **37%**; 1−(1−0.078)^12 = **62%** (arithmetic).
- **Structural churn:** “A test prep platform might retain users through weeks of study, only to see [them leave after the exam]” — [UserIntuition, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.userintuition.ai/reference-guides/vertical-deep-dive-education-and-edtech-churn-patterns/) [vendor analysis].
- **Up Learn:** access is sold **“until summer 2027 exams”**, i.e. the product has a built-in end date; the monthly plan is **£104.99/month, cancel anytime** — [Up Learn pricing](https://uplearn.co.uk/pricing) [company claim]. Trustpilot (4.4/5) contains a reviewer who says classmates using Up Learn as their **only** revision for A-level Biology/Chemistry got **D/E grades**, and calls the price “extortionate”; another says it was compulsory at their school and non-compliance was punished with detentions — [Trustpilot Up Learn](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/uplearn.co.uk) [user reviews, anecdotal].
- **Seneca:** a reviewer reports an **unauthorised pending charge of £179.88 on 3 October 2026** and says they did not sign up for an ongoing subscription; the company’s public reply and other reviews stress help with cancellation — [Trustpilot Seneca](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/senecalearning.com) [user report]. Seneca’s own help page confirms three packages with monthly, seasonal or annual renewal — [Seneca help](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3746290-how-much-is-premium) [company claim].
- **Save My Exams:** its join page warns that **“if you cancel on the end date this may be too late and you will be charged for the annual subscription”**, and that all subscriptions auto-renew — [Save My Exams join](https://www.savemyexams.com/join) [company claim]. Trustpilot rating 4.6 — [Kinescope, 7 Oct 2026](https://kinescope.com/edtech-map/savemyexams) [secondary].
- **Kumon:** 20% annual **franchisee** turnover — [freefdds](https://freefdds.com/compare/kumon-vs-mathnasium) [secondary]. This is not student churn, but it is a signal that even the most routinised parent-paid model has high operator attrition.
- **Tassomai:** school access runs **“until the beginning of the next academic year”**, i.e. a annual licence, not an open-ended subscription — [BESA LendED](https://www.lended.org.uk/product/tassomai) [secondary, quoting Tassomai].

### Inferences

- **The “exam burst then silence” is not a bug in these businesses; it is the business model.** They sell a campaign, not a habit. The habit (Kumon, Tassomai’s Daily Goal) is the _retention mechanism inside the campaign_; the campaign ends at the exam.
- **If the Course Pack is sold per course/term, it should be priced and renewed like a campaign**, with an explicit end date and a re-sell at the next course. A monthly “keep your memory fresh forever” subscription fights the natural churn curve and will lose.
- **Refund/auto-renew complaints are a pricing signal, not just a support issue.** The £179.88 Seneca charge is close to the top of what a UK family will accept for a self-serve revision tool without a human; above that, they expect a guarantee (Up Learn) or a centre (Kumon).
- **Completion is the missing metric.** None of these companies publishes completion of a course, only sign-ups and grades of self-selected completers. Up Learn’s 97% is a completion-conditional claim, not an intent-to-treat outcome.

### Gaps

- No audited student churn, refund rate or course-completion rate for Up Learn, Tassomai, Seneca, Save My Exams, Quizlet or AnkiHub.
- No public data on how many Up Learn buyers claim the money-back guarantee, or what the claim rate is.
- App-store review corpora were not systematically analysed; the Trustpilot evidence here is illustrative, not a sample.

## 6. What these businesses sell that a general chatbot cannot

### Takeaway

They sell five things a chatbot does not: **sequence, a schedule, diagnosis, a maintained artifact, and a credential/guarantee.** The explanation is the commodity; the wrapper is the product. This is the most important finding for the Course Pack thesis: the pack should be sold as **a sequence with an end date and a proof of coverage**, not as “explanations for a book”.

### Cited Findings

- **Sequence / exam-board mapping:** Up Learn maps every course to the exam specification and says “content mapped to your exam spec, no textbooks needed” — [Up Learn](https://uplearn.co.uk) [company claim]. Save My Exams organises by board, subject, paper and topic, with “teacher-created model answers that match exam-board mark schemes” — [Save My Exams](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/support/why-pay-for-save-my-exams) [company claim]. Tassomai says every topic is “directly matched to the specification” — [BESA LendED](https://www.lended.org.uk/product/tassomai) [secondary].
- **Schedule / accountability:** Kumon’s daily 30-minute worksheet and twice-weekly centre visit; Tassomai’s **Daily Goal** and weekly **parent email/dashboard**; Mathnasium’s recommended 2–3 visits per week — [Kumon UK](https://www.kumon.co.uk/helpdesk), [Tassomai school parents](https://www.tassomai.com/school-parents), [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/mathnasium-cost) [company claims / secondary].
- **Diagnosis:** Mathnasium’s free assessment and individualised learning plan; Tassomai’s knowledge “Tree” that shows strengths and gaps to teachers and parents; Up Learn’s “identifies your weaker areas” — [Mathnasium](https://www.mathnasium.com), [Tassomai](https://www.tassomai.com/tree), [EdTech Impact Up Learn review](https://edtechimpact.com/products/up-learn) [company claims].
- **Maintained artifact:** AnkiHub’s entire paid pitch is live updates and errata correction on a shared deck — [AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net) and [StudyCardsAI](https://studycardsai.com/blog/anking-deck-guide) [company claim / secondary].
- **Credential / guarantee:** Up Learn’s **“top grades guaranteed or your money back”** and 97% A*/A claim; Seneca’s **“Seneca Guarantee” at £5.99/month** — [Up Learn](https://uplearn.co.uk), [Seneca help](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/3746290-how-much-is-premium) [company claims].
- **Human support as a paid add-on:** Up Learn includes 24/7 tutor support; Tassomai caps AI-tutor interactions for school users and sells **“Mai Unlimited” at £3.99/month** to parents — [Up Learn](https://uplearn.co.uk), [Tassomai Mai Unlimited](https://www.tassomai.com/mai-unlimited) [company claims].

### Inferences

- **A general chatbot can produce an explanation on demand; it cannot certify coverage, enforce a schedule, or tell a parent what is missing.** The paid wrapper is therefore **coverage + cadence + diagnosis**, which is exactly what a “book turned into a course” can provide.
- **The Course Pack’s defensible claim is “this book, fully covered, in this order, with these checks, updated for this edition.”** That is closer to AnkiHub + Tassomai than to ChatGPT.
- **The buyer matters more than the feature.** Parents pay for accountability and diagnosis (Kumon, Mathnasium, Tassomai Parent Club); students pay for grade guarantees and time-saving (Up Learn, Save My Exams); schools pay for coverage and reporting (Dr Frost £650/school/year, Tassomai £5–15/student/year). A single €60/year student price leaves the parent and teacher budgets untouched.
- **A chatbot-native competitor can copy explanations but not the sequence, the mark-scheme alignment, or the maintenance.** The moat is editorial and institutional, not model quality.

### Gaps

- No data on how much of Up Learn’s value parents attribute to the guarantee vs the content; no pricing study isolating “sequence” from “explanation”.
- No evidence on whether teachers will pay for a book-specific pack, as opposed to a generic platform (Dr Frost) or a per-student licence (Tassomai). This is the central unknown for the Course Pack.

## 7. Willingness-to-pay anchor: household spend on private tutoring in the UK, Germany and the US

### Takeaway

Household spend is large and stable, and it dwarfs app pricing. In the UK, **29% of secondary pupils have ever had private tutoring (45% in London)**, at **£25–60/hour**; in Germany, the last rigorous national estimate is **€0.94–1.47 billion/year** (dated); in the US, parents report willingness to pay **$357/month** for tutoring. Even a conservative share of that spend supports a €60–300/year software product — but only if it is positioned as _part of the tutoring/teaching workflow_, not as a replacement for a tutor.

### Cited Findings

**UK**
- **29%** of secondary students in England and Wales have had private tutoring at some point; **11%** in the current year; **Year 11 is the peak at 25%**, Year 10 at 10%; **London 45% vs 27% rest of England**; **disadvantaged 23% vs best-off 30%** — [Sutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026](https://www.suttontrust.com/our-research/private-tutoring-2026), based on Ipsos Young People Omnibus 2025, 3,214 interviews aged 11–17, fieldwork 16 Jan–13 Jun 2025 — [Sutton Trust methodology PDF](https://www.suttontrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Private-Tutoring-2026.pdf).
- **20%** of pupils had one-to-one or small-group tutoring **in school**, down from 22% in 2023; **58% of schools reduced their tutoring offer in 2025** versus the previous year — [Sutton Trust 2026](https://www.suttontrust.com/our-research/private-tutoring-2026) [independent].
- Hourly rates: **£25–60/hour** broadly; **GCSE £35–50**, **A-level £40–60** — [Save My Exams tutor cost guide, 2025](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/revision-tips/how-much-does-a-tutor-cost) [secondary]; **£30–50/hour, ~£38 GCSE** — [Those Who Can](https://www.thosewhocan.org/tutor-rates-uk-calculator) [secondary]; GoStudent’s 2025/26 table gives **£17–38/hour** for lower-secondary to A-level depending on support level — [GoStudent](https://www.gostudent.org/en-gb/blog/tutor-pay-by-hour) [company data].
- The UK government’s tutoring programme (National Tutoring Programme) is referenced in school purchasing guides as **£6,000+ per institution** in catch-up funding, and Up Learn explicitly markets itself against it — [Up Learn funding guide](https://6752538.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/6752538/Up%20Learn%20-%20A%20guide%20to%20navigating%2016%20-%2019%20funding.pdf) [company claim; the funding figure is a DfE policy parameter, not a spend estimate].

**Germany**
- Parents spend **€942 million–€1.468 billion per year** on Nachhilfe, for **~1.1 million pupils**; average **€108–168 per pupil per year** across all pupils (not per tutoring user) — [Bertelsmann Stiftung / Klemm & Klemm](https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/fileadmin/files/BSt/Publikationen/GrauePublikationen/GP_Ausgaben_fuer_Nachhilfe.pdf) [independent, but based on 2007 pupil numbers and 2006 income data — dated]. Summary: [bildungsklick](https://bildungsklick.de/schule/detail/1-5-milliarden-euro-pro-jahr-fuer-nachhilfe) [secondary].
- **~€2 billion/year** industry estimate in 2015 — [Handelsblatt, 30 Aug 2015](https://www.handelsblatt.com/unternehmen/dienstleister/milliarden-geschaeft-nachhilfe-bueffeln-fuer-bessere-noten/12246640.html) [secondary, paywalled/old].
- Market structure: **~3,000 private Nachhilfe institutes**; Studienkreis ~1,000 locations and ~600,000 students at ~€70M revenue (older profile); Schülerhilfe ~900 locations across Germany, Austria and France — [Hamburger Abendblatt](https://www.abendblatt.de/wirtschaft/article106879024/Milliardengeschaeft-Nachhilfe.html) [secondary, old].
- Kumon Germany charges **€111/month per subject**, i.e. **€1,332/year** for one subject — [Kumon Neuss-Furth](https://www.kumon.de/neuss-furth) [company claim].
- **No current official German statistic** was found: the Statistisches Bundesamt’s consumption survey (EVS) does not publish a separate “Nachhilfe” line in the public tables reviewed — [Destatis consumption expenditure tables](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Gesellschaft-Umwelt/Einkommen-Konsum-Lebensbedingungen/Konsumausgaben-Lebenshaltungskosten/_inhalt.html) [official, but no tutoring breakdown].

**US**
- School parents report an average willingness to pay **$357/month** for tutoring (July 2025 survey wave) — [EdChoice, A Kaleidoscope View of K–12 Tutoring in America, 2025](https://www.edchoice.org/2025-a-kaleidoscope-view-of-k12-tutoring-in-america) [independent survey].
- US private tutoring market forecast to grow by **$28.85 billion over 2025–2029 at 11.1% CAGR** — [Technavio via PR Newswire, 24 Jan 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/private-tutoring-market-in-the-us-to-grow-by-usd-28-85-billion-2025-2029-driven-by-emphasis-on-stem-education-report-highlights-ai-driven-transformation---technavio-302358318.html) [vendor forecast]; Technavio’s 2026 update says **+$32.44 billion 2025–2030, 11.3% CAGR**, with curriculum-based tutoring **$23.56 billion in 2024** — [Technavio, May 2026](https://www.technavio.com/report/private-tutoring-market-in-us-industry-analysis) [vendor forecast].
- US **online** tutoring: **$13.53 billion in 2025 → $22.46 billion by 2031, 9.37% CAGR** — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-online-tutoring-services-market) [vendor forecast]. A separate report puts US online tutoring at **$4.32 billion in 2024 → $8.08 billion by 2030** — [Yahoo Finance/ResearchAndMarkets](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/u-online-private-tutoring-market-081500319.html) [vendor forecast]. **The forecasts disagree by ~3×; treat all as directional only.**
- Global private tutoring market: **$93.6 billion (2023)** — [Market.us](https://market.us/report/private-tutoring-market) vs **$133.8 billion (2025)** — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/private-tutoring-market). Again, vendor estimates, not a single source of truth.

### Inferences

- **Illustrative UK annual spend (inference, not a published figure):** 11% of ~3.3 million secondary pupils ≈ **360,000 current tutees**; at £38/hour × 1 hour/week × 30 weeks ≈ **£1,140 per tutee/year**; total ≈ **£410 million/year**. If “ever tutored” (29% ≈ 950,000) is used, the gross pool is ≈ **£1.1 billion/year**. Both are order-of-magnitude, and the assumptions (pupil count, intensity, rate) should be stated if used. The published Sutton Trust data supports the _penetration_, not the _spend_.
- **Germany’s €1–1.5 billion figure is the only rigorous national estimate, and it is ~15 years old.** The true 2026 number is probably higher (inflation, more tutoring, higher rates), but no source found justifies a specific updated figure. Use the old figure with an explicit date caveat.
- **US willingness to pay ($357/month) is ~70× a €60/year app.** Even if only 1 in 20 families who say that actually spend it, the tutoring pool is three orders of magnitude larger than the app pool.
- **The Course Pack should be priced against the tutoring hour, not the app store.** A £20–30 per-course pack is “less than one tutoring hour” and fits the £25–60/hour anchor; a €60/year subscription is “about one to two hours” and fits only if it replaces a revision guide, not a tutor.
- **The high-stakes trigger is strongest where the exam is external and dated** (GCSE/A-level, Abitur, SAT/ACT, NEET/JEE, Suneung). That is consistent with the current doc’s thesis, but the evidence says the _price_ attached to that trigger is 3–7× €60, not €60.

### Gaps

- No current, official German household spend figure; the Bertelsmann number is dated and the Destatis EVS does not break out tutoring.
- No UK official total spend on private tutoring; the Sutton Trust measures participation, not £.
- US market-size estimates are vendor forecasts that disagree by 3×; no government series.
- No data on what share of tutoring spend is already intermediated by software (booking, materials, assessment) — the actual addressable budget for a Course Pack.

## Cross-cutting implications for the market-research document

1. **€60/year is a commodity-tool price, not a high-stakes price.** The evidence supports €60/year only for a self-serve, no-guarantee, low-stakes product. Exam-prep products with a guarantee sell at **£180–430 per subject per exam cycle**; human centres at **£840–6,000/year**; the Indian mass market at **~₹8,000 (~$90)/year**.
2. **The buyer is usually the parent, not the student.** Kumon, Mathnasium, Tassomai Parent Club and the Sutton Trust data all point to household budgets. A per-seat Course Pack sold to “whoever teaches that book” should have a **teacher/department price** (compare Dr Frost £650/school/year, Tassomai £5–15/student/year) and a **parent price** (compare £70–111/month Kumon).
3. **Retention sells only when bundled with maintenance or a deadline.** AnkiHub proves people pay for a deck that stays correct; Tassomai proves they pay for a daily quota; Up Learn proves they pay for a guarantee. “FSRS inside” is not a price.
4. **The exam-burst pattern is structural.** Price and renew per course/exam cycle; do not model a perpetual monthly subscription.
5. **The moat is editorial and institutional.** Sequence, mark-scheme alignment, diagnosis and maintenance are what a chatbot cannot supply. The Course Pack should make those visible — coverage map, errata log, edition updates, a completion record — or it will be compared to ChatGPT and priced like Quizlet.
